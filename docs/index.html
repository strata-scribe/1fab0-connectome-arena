How It Works

A fruit fly tracks odors using dual antennae. By comparing the concentration between left and right, its brain computes steering turns toward food.

The Question: Does this behavior emerge from evolved biological wiring, or would any random network with the same number of neurons and connections do the same?

The Test: Two flies (Fly A and Fly B) start at the exact same location and heading. One runs the biological connectome circuit; the other runs a scrambled twin with identical parts but randomized wiring. To avoid bias, their identities are hidden until the flight finishes.
